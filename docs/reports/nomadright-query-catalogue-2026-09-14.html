How a garbled sentence still gets answered

There is no layer that survives a completely destroyed sentence: if nothing recognisable is left, the kiosk apologises rather than guess. What it does survive is a garbled word inside a sentence that still carries its meaning, and a sentence whose words differ from anything stored but whose meaning matches. The layers, in the order a spoken sentence passes through them:

  1. 1 · Kiosk questionsGreetings, thanks, "who are you", "what can you do", "how do I use this", "which languages", "can you fill a form", "which schemes". Matched on the native words and on the translated English, similarity ≥ 0.90 for greetings and thanks, ≥ 0.82 for the rest. Fixed answers in the person's language. Section 1.
  2. 2 · Native-script correctionHindi and Tamil only, before translation. A word that is not a known word is replaced by a scheme or welfare word with the same consonant skeleton (vowel signs, nasal marks and nukta ignored, look-alike consonants merged), equal or one edit apart, if the spelling is at least 45 % similar. Function words and greetings are never touched. A recognised scheme word also becomes a hint for the pipeline. Example: इनडिएस … महनदंड → पीडीएस … मानदंड. Section 2.
  3. 3 · TranslationIndicTrans2 turns the corrected sentence into English. The rest of the pipeline works in English; the answer is translated back, or spoken from a fixed native text.
  4. 4 · Sound-alike correctionEnglish side. A word that is not an English dictionary word is replaced by a scheme-name word with the same Indian-English sound key ("rashan" and "ration" → rsn, "kissan" → ksn, "ujwala" → ujvl) when it is at least 50 % similar, or by any alias word at least 84 % similar. "e sharam", "aayushman", "samridhi", "labour cart" all land. Real English words are left alone, so "cart" in "ration cart" is carried by the alias match instead.
  5. 5 · Scheme names34 schemes, 280 English aliases, plus the Hindi and Tamil spoken forms. A named scheme fixes the topic; a scheme named earlier in the conversation is remembered until Home. Section 2.
  6. 6 · Meaning match797 stored phrasings in 22 groups (multilingual sentence embeddings, 7 nearest neighbours, similarity ≥ 0.86, ≥ 55 % agreement) decide what kind of question it is and, when no scheme is named, which topic. 48 questions have a fixed written answer (≥ 0.93). Sections 3 and 4.
  7. 7 · SituationsAge, origin state, current place, work, family and ration category are read from the sentence and fed to 40 eligibility rules and the scheme finder. Section 5.
  8. 8 · Clarify, then apologiseA bare "card", "documents", "how to apply", "pension", "I need money" gets one clarifying question. What is left with no scheme, no topic and no situation gets the apology (or the model, when QUERY_FALLBACK is "qwen").

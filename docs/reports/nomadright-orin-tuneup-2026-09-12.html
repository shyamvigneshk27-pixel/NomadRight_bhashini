1 · Tamil ASR: which model, and why

Four runtimes of the Tamil models on this device were benchmarked on the same clips: the Conformer-CTC TensorRT engine the service used, the Whisper-medium fine-tune from ~/onnx-int8 run three ways (ONNX Runtime CPU as delivered, the TensorRT engines built for it, and CTranslate2), plus Hindi Conformer as the reference for a healthy engine. Sets: FLEURS test read speech (real recordings, 150 Tamil / 60 Hindi clips), 20 kiosk-domain sentences synthesised with the device's own voices, and noisy (10 dB SNR) and 1.15× fast copies. Conclusion. The Tamil Conformer is not usable (three of four words wrong on every set, even the clean kiosk sentences). The Whisper fine-tune is accurate on all Tamil sets and degrades gracefully with noise; only speech sped up 15 % pushes it to WER 0.4. It is Tamil-only: Hindi and English audio come out transliterated into Tamil script (the fine-tune ignores the language token), so Hindi stays on Conformer and English ASR is not offered. TensorRT cut Whisper's latency 6× against the delivered ONNX-Runtime build (1.6 s vs 9.3 s per kiosk sentence) at 13 % CPU instead of 99 %, with the same accuracy band. The same weights were also converted to CTranslate2 (int8 weights, FP16 compute, 739 MB on disk instead of 1.65 GB of engines; scripts/onnx_to_hf.py rebuilds a Hugging Face checkpoint from the INT8 export first). Measured with the service stopped: WER 0.069 on kiosk sentences and 0.118 on FLEURS (TensorRT 0.047 / 0.121), noisy 0.170 vs 0.182; 2.57 s vs 1.58 s per kiosk sentence and 5.16 s vs 4.64 s on 10-second clips; process peak RSS 2677 MB vs 4036 MB. A first CT2 run scored WER 0.46 - that was a wrapper bug (CTranslate2 counts max_length differently and cut every clip at 66 tokens), found by looking at the per-clip outputs and fixed before this measurement. So: TensorRT for latency (installed), CTranslate2 when memory matters more (asr_config.json → "ta": "whisper-ct2").

What the conversion took

  • The ONNX export is ORT dynamic-INT8 (MatMulInteger); TensorRT cannot run it, so it was rewritten to weight-only dequantise nodes, then folded to FP16 constants (conv weights kept FP32).
  • Builds on the 8 GB device need free physical memory: bhashini stopped, caches dropped, one builder thread, 768 MB workspace, native tactics only.
  • Three runtime bugs found and fixed by measurement: a Fortran-ordered log-mel array (cosine 0.71 against the reference until ascontiguousarray), a TensorRT fusion of the final LayerNorm into the LM head that produced NaN logits (kept as an extra engine output), and a zero-length KV cache whose null address TensorRT rejects.
  • Lighter engine layout installed: cross-attention K/V projections as a 96 MB engine plus a with-past decoder that accepts an empty cache, dropping the 1 GB prompt decoder. Identical transcripts on 12/12 clips, same latency, peak RSS 3.4 GB instead of 4.3 GB, load 16 s instead of 29 s. Files: ~/onnx-int8-tensorRT/engines (see LAYOUT.md), runtime bhashini_models/asr/whisper_trt.py, selection in asr/asr_config.json.

2 · Language selection and the interface

The start page offers English, Hindi and Tamil. Choosing one now does three things at once: the interface strings switch (every label, button, status line and backend message goes through one trilingual table, src/i18n/strings.ts plus a localiser for backend text), the microphone language is set so ASR only decodes that language (select_asr_lang → the app's ASR <lang> setting; the service transcribes with that language's engine only), and answers are spoken and shown in it. English is offered for the screen but the microphone stays on Hindi/Tamil because there is no English ASR on the device (the UI shows that, greyed, rather than pretending). A language change or the Home button also clears the previous person's facts and scheme context (app._reset_conversation), which the tough tests showed leaking between visitors.

Needs a native speaker's eye: the Hindi and Tamil interface strings and the scheme catalogue translations were produced by the on-device NMT and checked only for consistency, not idiom (e.g. "PM-Kisan நிறுவனம்" in the catalogue).

3 · Government-website style

All motion removed (transitions disabled globally, no entrance effects, no gradients), one 4 px radius, two font weights per script, plain high-contrast panels, big touch targets, status expressed in words not colour alone. The admin terminal (xterm) is lazy-loaded so the kiosk pages ship 400 KB less JavaScript; the camera preview is downscaled to 640 px before it crosses the websocket. Screenshots were taken headlessly for every screen in all three languages via the #screen=X&lang=y deep link.

4 · Ollama vs llama.cpp for Qwen3-VL-2B

Both serve the identical GGUF and vision projector from Ollama's blob store, so the comparison isolates the serving layer. Ollama 0.32.6 runs its bundled llama-server but fixes the vision encoder's minimum at 1,048,576 pixels (≈1,230 image tokens per photo) and exposes no image-token controls; the same binary started directly accepts --image-min-tokens/--image-max-tokens and KV-cache quantisation. Conclusion. Ollama hard-codes the vision encoder's minimum at 1,048,576 pixels, so every photo becomes ~1,215 tokens whatever size the app sends (the 512-px and 1024-px rows are identical) and prompt evaluation alone costs 1.97 s; generation runs at 21 tok/s. The same llama-server started directly (with its CUDA backend on GGML_BACKEND_PATH — without that it silently runs on the CPU at 10 tok/s, which cost one whole benchmark run) sizes the image to its real resolution: prompt evaluation 0.38 s, 23 tok/s, a fresh photo with the full prompt and a 100-token budget in 2.6 s median / 5.0 s worst, the short prompt in 0.9 s. Capping images at 512 tokens changes latency by nothing measurable (2.7 s) and saves 331 MB; q8 KV cache another 393 MB (runner 3282 MB vs Ollama's 4532 MB, benchmark's own measure). Answer text and decline behaviour were the same across all four (7/72 vs 6/72 declines on the same 72 calls). Ollama's advantages are operational only: the systemd unit, model store and keep-alive/unload management the app already relies on; the app would need a small serving shim (OpenAI-style /v1/chat/completions instead of /api/generate, and its own load/unload policy).

Next action is yours. Per your instruction nothing has been switched: the app still talks to Ollama on :11434. Done (evening of 12 September): the app serves Qwen through its own on-demand llama-server (nomad_right/llama_server.py, -ngl 99 -c 2048 --image-max-tokens 512 -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0, CUDA backend via GGML_BACKEND_PATH), started on the Camera/Ask-Chatbot press and stopped five minutes after its last answer; the Ollama path stays behind LLM_BACKEND = "ollama" for rollback. Verified: identical answers and declines on the same prompts (4/6 declines on both), a fresh photo answered in 5.2 s and a second in 2.7 s under memory pressure from a standalone process. Tamil ASR was moved to the CTranslate2 build at the same time (bhashini 3.4 GB instead of 4.6 GB after inference). Camera answers under 3 s

Measured, not assumed: with Ollama, a fresh photo cannot be answered under 3 s with the current prompt and 100-token budget (5.6 s median; the ~2 s image prompt is fixed). The ‘answer quickly’ pre-prompt (≤20 words, 40-token budget) brings it to 1.8 s when the image is already cached and roughly 2.6–3.1 s cold — borderline — and its answers are terser but still correct on the document images (card type, issuing state, UAN, blank fields). With llama-server the same short prompt answers in 0.9 s and even the full prompt stays under 3 s at the median (2.6 s), so the <3 s target is met without shortening the prompt. Nothing in the camera pipeline was changed: the choice between (a) Ollama + short prompt + 40 tokens (borderline) and (b) llama-server (comfortable) is yours; the OCR→table→text→NMT→TTS alternative was not needed and is not proposed — its NMT+TTS tail alone (measured 1.0 s + 1.9 s per turn) would exceed what the vision model now takes.

Where the memory goes: bhashini is 1.5 GB of CPU-side heap and libraries (torch, TensorRT, CTranslate2, Python) plus about 3 GB of GPU-side allocations that smaps does not show — Whisper engines 1.65 GB, the Hindi Conformer, two IndicTrans2 CT2 models (214 MB each on disk, loaded on CUDA in background threads right after start-up), and three CUDA contexts. The Qwen runner is 2.3 GB with the vision projector and a 2048-token context. The kiosk app is 0.85 GB at steady state.

What was changed to get under the line

  • Whisper engine layout (−0.9 GB vs the first build): described in section 1.
  • NMT: only en↔indic pre-loaded; the 326 MB indic↔indic model loads on first use (voice bridge).
  • Qwen residency: LLM_KEEP_ALIVE 30 min → 5 min, and the model is pre-loaded in the background the moment the Camera button is pressed (app._prewarm_qwen → qwen_client.prewarm()), so the photo question still meets a warm model while the 2.3 GB is not held for half an hour after every visitor. Ollama reloads the model in about 0.5 s while its files are still in the page cache (the benchmark's load_ms); after other work has evicted them the reload is disk-bound and the earlier team note measured about 45 s - which is why the pre-load now starts on the Camera press rather than on the question.
  • Spoken answers capped at 45 words on the legacy path (60 words measured as 21–23 s of flite speech).
  • Tamil ASR on CTranslate2 (optional, one config line): 1359 MB less than the TensorRT engines at +1.0 s per utterance - see section 1.
Where this stands. The budget is 7.6 GB, of which the kernel, display and desktop take about 0.9 GB. bhashini needs 4.2-4.7 GB with the TensorRT Whisper (3.6 GB after start-up plus what inference touches), the kiosk app 0.85-1.1 GB, WebKit 0.2-0.3 GB - about 6.3 GB before any language model, leaving roughly 1.3 GB. The Qwen runner needs 1.8-2.7 GB under Ollama, so a camera session or a model-answered voice question overshoots by 0.5-1.4 GB and the swap file starts moving pages; the stale pages then make every later turn slow until they are paged back. What gets it under the line, all measured in this report: (1) serve Qwen with llama-server, 512-token images and a q8 KV cache - the runner drops to about 1.5 GB (3.3 vs 4.5 GB by the benchmark's own measure), which alone brings a camera session to about 6.5 GB; (2) switch Tamil to the CTranslate2 build when memory matters more than the second it adds per utterance (asr_config.json: 'ta': 'whisper-ct2', -0.7 GB); (3) keep voice turns away from Qwen (LLM_FALLBACK_ENABLED): the scheme-intelligence layer answered 40 of the 42 tough cases without it and the two model-written answers become template answers - then voice operation never loads the 2 GB at all and only camera sessions do, pre-loaded on the button press. Done today: 5-minute Qwen residency with pre-load on Camera, the lighter Whisper engine layout, NMT lazy-loading, and the stale swap drained after the tests. None of the three remaining levers was applied without you: (1) is the switch you asked to decide, (2) trades a second of latency, (3) changes what two rare questions are told.
